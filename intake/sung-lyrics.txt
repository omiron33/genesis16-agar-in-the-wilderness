Sara, Abram’s wife, bore no children.
She had an Egyptian maid named Agar.
Sara said to Abram, “The Lord has kept me
from having children. Go to my maid,
so I may have children through her.”
Abram listened to Sara.
After Abram had lived ten years in Chanaan,
Sara took Agar the Egyptian, her maid,
and gave her to Abram as his wife.
He went in to Agar, and she conceived.
When she knew she was pregnant,
she looked down on her mistress.
Sara said to Abram, “This wrong is your fault.
I gave my maid to you, and now that she is pregnant,
I am dishonored before her. Let the Lord judge
between you and me.”
Abram said to Sara, “Your maid is in your hands.
Treat her as you think best.”
Sara afflicted Agar, and she fled from her.
The Lord’s angel found her by a spring
in the wilderness, on the road to Sur.
The angel said, “Agar, Sara’s maid,
where have you come from, and where are you going?”
She said, “I am fleeing from my mistress Sara.”
The Lord’s angel said,
“Return to your mistress;
submit yourself under her hands.”
The Lord’s angel said,
“I will greatly multiply your descendants;
they will be too many to count.”
The Lord’s angel said, “You are with child;
you will bear a son. Name him Ismael,
for the Lord has heard your humiliation.”
“He will be a wild man;
his hands against all, all hands against him.
He will live before all his brothers.”
She named the Lord God who spoke to her,
“You are God who sees me.”
She said, “I have openly seen
him who appeared to me.”
She called it “The well of him
whom I have openly seen.”
It lies between Cades and Barad.
Agar bore Abram a son,
and Abram named the boy Ismael.
Abram was eighty-six years old
when Agar bore Ismael to him.

# Genesis 16, Agar in the Wilderness: director brief

**Mode:** code-only GLSL at 1920×1080 and 60 fps. 8 samples, or 16 on the fast-motion scenes (the
veil torn away, the stars, the wind over the dunes, the eye opening, the wide shot of the eye). Built
the Genesis 8 way: short scenes cut on beats, one design system, lyrics as their own sharp type layer,
one fast draft and one critic pass.

## Design system

- **People:** none are drawn. Agar is present only through her traces.
  - The indigo veil on its line by the small tent; the wind tears it away; it lies by the spring;
    at the end it hangs on its line again.
  - Footprints the wind is filling: from her tent to Abram's, out over the dunes, down to the spring,
    and back home at the end.
  Sara and Abram are their tents and their lamps.
- **The angel of the Lord:** light only. A small, steady core over the water, its reflection, and a
  soft glow in the air. No figure and no wings.
- **Running motif: being seen.**
  - The spring is an eye of water in the desert.
  - At "You are God who sees me", the cloud deck opens into an almond with the sun as its pupil, and
    its light runs out over the sand to the spring.
  - The pool reflects the eye.
- **Palette that builds:**
  1. Dusty amber afternoon (the household).
  2. Hard white noon (the quarrel).
  3. Burnt heat (the flight).
  4. Cool teal dusk (the spring).
  5. Indigo night (the promise).
  6. Dawn gold (El Roi).
  7. Evening amber into night (the return and the child).
- **Signal colour:** the light that sees (255, 214, 150). It appears on the angel's light, the
  opening eye, and the angel's words.
- **Two typefaces:** EB Garamond for every sung word, and Inter Tight in tracked capitals for the
  annotation (GENESIS 16:v, YEAR n, EIGHTY-SIX YEARS).
- **Text in daylight:** dark ink on the bright day scenes. Light type is never set on a bright sky.
- **Theology:** the angel of the Lord speaks as the Lord, so his words take the signal gold.
  - Agar's confession "You are God who sees me" is the climax.
  - The return and the submission are sung soberly. The film does not romanticise the coercion of
    verses 2 to 4: that is a night with the small tent dark.
